# Evidence Review: What Actually Survives Costs in Crypto Spot Trading

Purpose: graded findings for the six research questions, written to seed the nightly LLM research loop and to re-baseline the gates of a Kraken spot, long-only, 4h/1d Freqtrade system.
Date: 2026-10-14
How to read the grades: **A** = replicated across independent studies, out-of-sample, after realistic costs. **B** = one solid study (peer-reviewed or well-documented), usually in-sample or gross of costs, or replicated but not in crypto. **C** = anecdotal, vendor, blog, preprint-only, or in-sample only. "Unknown" means no usable public source was found, not that the effect is disproven.

---

## 0. Read this first: the fee assumption is in conflict and changes every number downstream

The system's gates were calibrated to 0.16% maker / 0.26% taker. One research note fetched Kraken's official fee-schedule page on 2026-10-14 and reports **Tier 1 (< $2,500 30-day volume) at 0.40% maker / 0.80% taker** ([Kraken fee schedule](https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule)); Kraken's support page states fees range "from -0.02% (maker) to 0.80% (taker)" ([Kraken support](https://support.kraken.com/articles/201893638-fee-schedule)). A second note reached the same 0.40/0.80 figure via a third-party explainer (updated 2026-08-24) which says the cross-platform tier change "took effect on July 9, 2026" and that the prior entry rate was 0.25/0.40 ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/kraken-fees-explained)). Three other notes state that the 0.16/0.26 assumption was *not* verified in their pass, and one note reports the Kraken `AssetPairs` API returned empty `fees` arrays ([Kraken AssetPairs](https://api.kraken.com/0/public/AssetPairs?pair=XBTUSD,SOLUSD,XRPUSD,LINKUSD,LTCUSD)).

**This is a conflict to verify first, not a settled fact.** The official page is the primary source and was fetched on the research date; the 9 July 2026 effective date comes only from a third party; whether the schedule applies identically to a Canadian account (`/ca/` fee page) was not checked; and 0.16/0.26 does not appear in any fetched Kraken document. Until the operator logs into the account and reads the fee tier shown on a live order ticket, every gate should be evaluated at **both** fee levels. A $100 account cannot reach Tier 2 ($2,500 30-day volume) without turning over 25× per month, so Tier 1 should be assumed permanent.

**Consequence at both fee levels** (round-trip cost as a fraction of notional; slippage is an assumption of 0.10% per side because no Kraken fill-rate or slippage data was found; the fee-ratio column assumes the gate is "fees paid ÷ gross profit < 40%"):

| Order pattern | Fees at 0.16/0.26 | + 0.10%/side slippage | Fees at 0.40/0.80 | + 0.10%/side slippage | Avg gross profit/trade needed for fee ratio < 40% (fees only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maker in / maker out | 0.32% | 0.52% | 0.80% | 1.00% | 0.80% → **2.00%** |
| Maker in / taker out (limit entry, stop-loss exit) | 0.42% | 0.62% | 1.20% | 1.40% | 1.05% → **3.00%** |
| Taker in / taker out | 0.52% | 0.72% | 1.60% | 1.80% | 1.30% → **4.00%** |

Two further facts bear on this. Freqtrade backtests use the exchange's ccxt default fee unless `--fee` is set, so a backtest run on Binance data silently applies ~0.1% ([Freqtrade backtesting](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)), a 4–8× understatement at Tier 1. And stop-loss exits are almost always taker orders, so the "maker-preferred" design only halves the entry leg. At the high fee level a 50%-hit-rate, 1:1 win/loss strategy can never pay for itself; the required average gross edge per trade (2–4%) is a multi-day move on these assets, which is why the evidence below on trade frequency matters more than any signal.

**Trend at weekly scale is the only return effect with more than one independent, after-cost data point, and even it is weakening.** Han, Kang & Ryu mark portfolios daily on 2013–2023 data, charge a flat 15 bp per trade, and find the best long-when-top-tercile / cash-otherwise rule (28-day lookback, 5-day hold) delivers Sharpe 1.51 after costs versus ~0.85 for the market while invested only ~48% of the time, beating the market in 8 of 10 years ([Han, Kang & Ryu](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)). The authors concede the (28,5) pair was chosen ex post on the full sample, with no separate OOS test. Liu & Tsyvinski show BTC's past 1-week return predicts the next 1–3 weeks (t = 3.7–4.5), but their returns are gross, the effect roughly halves when 2011–2012 is dropped, and the 1-day effect does not survive bootstrap ([NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)). Mercik, Zaremba & Demir's 36-predictor zoo on 2018–2024 finds 7-day momentum still positive at 0.4%/trade but losing ~70 points of annualised return to costs, and "much more profitable in 2018–2021" than after ([Crypto Factor Zoo](https://open.icm.edu.pl/bitstreams/21636cf6-61df-4fb5-9ae4-4195d592cd26/download)). Grobys et al. find plain weekly momentum on the top-30 coins earned 1.74%/week to July 2020 (10% significance only) and was negative and insignificant from August 2020 to December 2023, with one observation accounting for 37% of compounded return and tail exponents of 2.45–2.87 (variance undefined) ([Grobys et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). The honest reading: 7–28-day long-only TSMOM on BTC/large caps plausibly survives 0.3–0.5% round-trip; it has not been shown to survive 1.0–1.8%; and no study tests 4h bars on spot.

**Monthly-horizon and MA-crossover versions are not supported.** Grobys & Sapkota's monthly 1/6/12-month TSMOM on 143 coins is insignificant at 5% ([Vaasa PDF](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/397c4059-360e-46e6-937f-f80054ae6180/content)). Rozario et al. optimise SMA/EMA/DEMA crossovers on hourly Bitstamp data 2011–2019 to in-sample Sharpes of 1.1–1.35, then find walk-forward with one-year training "not robust" with "no predictable, attractive Sharpe ratios" and "intraday strategies were not profitable" ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.12155)). That is the single strongest warning in this literature for a loop that re-optimises nightly, and it is consistent with the system's own EMA(20/50) result (−2.8%, 65–72% drawdown over two years). The only OOS tracking of a Donchian-type rule (buy the 10–50-day high) is Quantpedia's cost-free, figures-only extension through August 2024, where "MAX still works" but the mean-reversion leg "performed poorly" ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)) — grade C.

**Volatility scaling stabilises; it does not add return, and the capped (≤ 1×) version is untested.** Man Group scales BTC to a 30% vol target over 2012–2024 and reports "around 40 Sharpe points" of improvement regardless of estimator half-life, with no drawdown table and no costs ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/crypto-too-hot-to-handle)). Grobys et al. find vol-managed crypto momentum cuts kurtosis from 121.8 to 68–106 and raises mean returns but "did not materially alter tail risk" ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). The replicated, after-cost evidence is non-crypto: Harvey et al. show vol targeting lifts equity Sharpe from ~0.40 to 0.48–0.51 at 1–3.5 bp costs, consistently cuts extreme returns across 60+ assets, but has negligible effect on currencies and commodities ([Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). For a long-only, no-leverage spot system, scaling can only reduce exposure in high-vol periods, so the Man uplift will not transfer in full; no source tests the ≤ 1× case, and this should be stated as unknown.

**Cross-sectional ranking, short-term reversion, calendar rules and regime models add nothing this system can trade.** Han et al. find CS momentum "weak" (5 of 21 portfolios liquidated), with profits from the long leg and crashes from single-coin outliers; Gbadebo's 8-coin test attributes a 55% CS drawdown partly to "high correlations among the coins" ([VU journal](https://www.journals.vu.lt/BATP/en/article/view/44540)). With five coins at ρ ≈ 0.55–0.75, ranking collapses to a TSMOM filter with extra turnover. The reversal premium that does exist is a small-coin liquidity-provision reward; Zaremba et al. state explicitly that "the largest, most tradeable coins show daily momentum rather than reversal" ([repository](https://earsiv.medeniyet.edu.tr/items/23a96c9f-3952-4bdf-aec5-abafb3aaab70/full)). The BTC Monday effect is the cleanest documented case of post-publication decay — significant on 2010–2017 data, gone after 2015 per Mueller's 2024 re-test ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v64y2024ics1544612324004598.html)). Markov-switching models improve one-step forecasts over a random walk but no study converts that into after-cost long-only P&L ([Koki et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03741)). The halving "cycle" has n = 4 with one failure: BTC set its all-time high of **$126,080 on 6 October 2025**, inside the post-halving year, yet 2025 closed roughly **−6%**, the first negative post-halving year ([Yahoo/CCN](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bitcoin-records-first-ever-negative-151143427.html); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/is-bitcoin-four-year-cycle-dead-2025)).

**Funding and carry are usable only defensively.** Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov document crypto carry averaging 7–10%/yr, spiking above 40–60%, driven by retail leveraged-long demand, and report that "high carry predicts future price crashes" ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)); their CEPR column adds that a 10% rise in standardised carry predicts a 22% rise in sell liquidations relative to open interest over the next month, but tests no spot-return rule ([CEPR](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/crypto-carry-market-segmentation-and-price-distortions-digital-asset-markets)). The only public funding-reversion backtest (BitMEX, 524 eight-hour periods) shows 44–53% hit rates and "no significant predictive power" ([BitMEX](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/xbtusd-funding-mean-reversion-strategy)). The supported use is "do not add longs when funding is at multi-month highs," not "buy negative funding." Funding history is free without a key from Binance (`/fapi/v1/fundingRate`, ≤ 1,000 rows per request) ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Get-Funding-Rate-History)) and Kraken Futures (`/historical-funding-rates`, depth undocumented) ([Kraken Futures docs](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/futures-api/trading/historical-funding-rates)).

**Correlation: the five pairs are one exposure.** The only dated public matrix is CME Group's daily log-return correlations for 2 Jan 2024–12 Sep 2025: **SOL–BTC 0.75, XRP–BTC 0.55**, ETH–BTC 0.76, XRP–SOL 0.55 ([CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/articles/2025/diversifying-crypto-portfolios-with-xrp-and-sol.html)). **LINK and LTC: unknown** — no published 2022–2026 figure was found; compute them from the Kraken archive. No bear-market (2022–23) matrix was found, and correlations are regime-dependent. The practical implication is that "max 1–2 positions" does not diversify; the risk budget should be set as for a single high-beta BTC position.

**Market context for backtest windows.** BTC peaked at $126,080 on 6 Oct 2025, fell >13% on 11 Oct 2025 with ~$19.2B of liquidations ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/btc-falls-34-from-2025-high-as-1011-crash-enters-its-first-year)), closed 2025 down ~6%, broke below its 200-day MA in November 2025, spent 189 days below it (vs 85–132 in prior cycles), printed a cycle low near **$60k in February 2026**, retested a *falling* 200DMA near $82k in May 2026 ([The Block / K33](https://www.theblock.co/post/401991/not-all-200-day-moving-averages-are-equal-k33-argues-februarys-60k-low-still-marks-cycles-maximum-drawdown)), and was ~$82.7k, 34% below the high, on 11 Oct 2026 ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/btc-falls-34-from-2025-high-as-1011-crash-enters-its-first-year)). Two consequences: a two-year window ending October 2026 contains one bull leg, one −52% drawdown and a year of chop under a declining MA, which is a fair but short test of a long-flat rule; and a loop that evaluates only on 2023–2025 will systematically under-value trend filters and over-value buy-and-hold-like exposure, because the benefit of long-flat trend is concentrated in 2018/2022/2025–26-type bears. Both bears must be in every evaluation window.

**Every public live test of autonomous LLM trading has a majority of losers, and rank order does not persist.** In nof1's Alpha Arena Season 1 (Hyperliquid perps, six models, $10,000 each), Qwen3 Max finished +22.3% and DeepSeek +4.9% while Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4 and GPT-5 lost 42–59% ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)); a second snapshot gives different loser percentages, so treat the decimals as uncertain ([iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/)). BTC buy-and-hold over the window was roughly flat (closes $107,198 on 18 Oct and $109,556 on 31 Oct) ([StatMuse](https://www.statmuse.com/money/ask/price-of-bitcoin-october-2025)). Interim leaderboards reversed within days, positions ran at 8–20× leverage, and DeepSeek gave back most of a +35% lead ([Blockworks](https://empire-blockworks.beehiiv.com/p/ai-trades-on-hyperliquid)). In Season 1.5 the S1 winner lost 29%, seven of eight models lost, one model placed 1,418 trades in two weeks under the same prompt that produced 158 from another, and the organiser's founder concluded autonomous LLM trading "isn't a workable path yet" ([Wallstreetcn](https://wallstreetcn.com/articles/3771681)). No per-model fee totals, drawdowns or Sharpes were ever published, no independent party reconciled the wallets, and no Season 2 results were found as of 2026-10-14. These arenas ran on a venue charging 0.045% taker ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees)), 9–18× cheaper than Kraken Tier 1, so they are optimistic relative to this system.

**The academic agent literature's positive results do not survive independent replication.** Profit Mirage tests FinMem, TradingAgents, FinAgent, FinCon and QuantAgent on Q2–Q3 2021 (inside GPT-4o's training window) versus Q3–Q4 2024 (after it) with similar market returns and finds 50–72% total-return decay and 51–62% Sharpe decay; frontier models answered ~85–93% of historical price/trend/event questions correctly, and LoRA fine-tuning raised in-distribution accuracy while cutting OOS accuracy ([arXiv 2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1)). The Alpha Illusion reproduces TradingAgents (Sharpe 0.43 → 0.22 after costs, below buy-and-hold on 4 of 5 stocks) and QuantAgent (−0.96 → −1.15) and notes that reported Sharpe standard errors are often as large as the Sharpes themselves ([alphaXiv 2605.16895](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16895v1)). FINSABER re-runs FinMem over 2000–2024 and 100+ symbols and gets 19.9% on TSLA in the originally reported window versus the published 61.8% ([arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)). All replications are US equities; no crypto live-money LLM-agent study exists.

**Open-source bots and marketplaces have no verifiable record, and professional funds are not a reassuring benchmark.** NostalgiaForInfinity's README makes no live-performance claim after five years and seven versions, recommends 5-minute bars with 6–12 open trades across 40–80 pairs, and is funded by referral links ([NFI GitHub](https://github.com/iterativv/NostalgiaForInfinity)); Freqtrade's own docs say backtesting "will never replace" dry-run and list fill assumptions (no slippage, fills anywhere inside the candle, stops at exact price) that flatter every published backtest ([Freqtrade backtesting](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)). Galaxy's opt-in VisionTrack Quantitative index lost 26.6% in 2022 and 7.5% in 2025 and is −0.4% YTD August 2026 against BTC at −64%, −6% and −4.5% respectively; the only consistently positive line is market-neutral (basis/arbitrage), which a $100 spot account cannot run ([VisionTrack](https://visiontrack.galaxy.com/indices/)). Opt-in GP reporting with T+60 revisions means these are upper bounds on the average realised result.

**The base rate is low single digits.** Taiwan's exchange data show fewer than 1% of ~360,000 day traders consistently profitable net of costs and >75% quitting within two years ([Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trade%20040330.pdf)); Brazil's futures day-traders show 3% with any net profit and 1.1% above minimum wage ([SSRN 3423101](https://ssrn.com/abstract=3423101)); India's regulator found 93% of >10 million F&O traders lost, with "most of the profits … generated by larger entities that used trading algorithms" — 96–97% of institutional profits were algorithmic, and transaction costs were ~28% of aggregate net losses ([SEBI via Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/fo-frenzy-over-93-retail-investors-lost-average-rs-2-lakh-in-three-years-says-sebi)); the BIS finds ~75% of retail bitcoin-app users across 95 countries lost money, with larger investors selling to smaller ones before declines ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2023-02-20-majority-of-bitcoin-retail-investors-lost-money-in-the-last-seven-years-bis-213372)). The discount rules that follow: vendor or blog returns with no statement or ledger carry zero evidential weight; organiser-run arenas with public wallets are B at best and have no statistical power at n ≈ 6–8 over two weeks; any LLM backtest inside the model's training window should be assumed 50–70% memorisation and then halved again for costs; any Freqtrade backtest that has not passed `lookahead-analysis`, `recursive-analysis` and `--timeframe-detail` should be assumed to exploit a fill assumption. What the few survivors shared was low turnover and explicit risk limits, never a better signal.

**The literature does not support the lesson that "exit logic matters most"; it supports the opposite.** Kaminski & Lo prove that under a random walk with positive premium π a stop-loss "will always reduce the portfolio's expected return," and that under AR(1) dynamics a stop adds value only when the autocorrelation ρ at the stop's horizon exceeds π/σ, the Sharpe ratio at that frequency ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). Empirically on S&P futures, "shorter term, lower frequency stop-loss policies have negative stopping premiums over large ranges of parameters"; only horizons above about a month are positive, and that is before costs (κ = 0). Clare et al., at 20 bp per side — the closest cost regime to this system in the literature — find a 12% trailing stop on a 200-day breakout *lowers* return and Sharpe (10.61%/0.56 → 10.13%/0.54), purchase-cost stops are unchanged or worse, the 50/200 crossover with a stop drops from 10.62% to 7.45%, and end-of-month evaluation beats daily evaluation (daily 10-day MA rule: −5.37%/yr, Sharpe −0.79) ([City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/17842/8/BLACKBOX%20%20%20SSRN-id2126476.pdf)). Their conclusion, "a change of trend is the best stop loss," is the single most on-point finding for a system whose EMA cross plus fixed 2×ATR stop lost 2.8% with a 65–72% drawdown. The one crypto result pointing the other way — Bui & Nguyen's ablation where removing an ATR(2.5×) trail cuts Sharpe 2.41 → 1.68 — is on 6-hour perpetuals across 150+ names at 4 bp, with Sharpe already falling to 1.62 at 12 bp and never tested at 20+ bp ([arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)). The two papers in which stops help (Han–Zhou–Zhu; Kaminski–Lo monthly) both park stopped capital in a positive-carry asset, gross of costs; on Kraken spot the stop asset is zero-carry USD and each stop costs a round trip.

**Drawdown is an exposure problem before it is an exit problem.** The only crypto-specific decomposition found — Summitward's BTC/ETH/SOL backtest at 1–4 bp — reports buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.73 / MDD −84%, raw trend without vol scaling Sharpe 0.71 / MDD −80%, vol-managed buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.74 / MDD −17%, and trend plus vol scaling Sharpe 0.79 / MDD −15%: "most of the drawdown reduction comes from volatility scaling, not the trend signal" ([Summitward](https://summitward.com/learn/crypto-trend-following)). This is a vendor's self-reported result with internal inconsistencies (BTC B&H Sharpe is 1.13 in one table and 0.73 in another), so grade C, but it is consistent with the system's own experience (a daily trend rule without vol scaling did not cut the drawdown) and with Bui & Nguyen's BTC buy-and-hold benchmark (MDD −64.1% over 2022–24). The replicated mechanism is Harvey et al.'s: in assets with a leverage effect, vol scaling behaves like time-series momentum and cuts vol-of-vol (US equities 4.6% → 1.8%) at turnover of at most ~5 round trips per year ([Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)); whether BTC behaves like equities or like the null-result commodities is an inference, not a finding. Fixed-fractional sizing ("risk 1–2% per trade with a 2×ATR stop") on a 70–80%-vol asset puts 25–100% of equity in one BTC position — exactly the exposure that produced the 65–72% drawdown — whereas sizing by volatility (fractional Kelly logic: half-Kelly keeps ~75% of growth with a 0.89 chance of doubling before halving, and mean-estimation errors matter ~20× more than variance errors ([MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf))) says hold 15–30% of equity in the asset.

**Profit targets, time stops, breakeven stops, equity-curve filters and drawdown tiers are unsupported or unknown.** Oxfordstrat's sweep of profit targets from 2% to 100% on 42 futures markets with costs concludes targets "reduce both absolute and risk-adjusted returns" ([Oxfordstrat](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=5178)); the lone counter-example is gross, on mean-reverting equity ETFs ([StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2023-05-do-profit-targets-add-value-to-746/)). BTC's return is concentrated in right-tail runs, so any cap on the upside removes the part of the distribution that pays for the losers. Time stops and breakeven stops have no cost-aware evidence in any asset class — say "untested," not "disproven." Equity-curve filters mostly lower return with marginal drawdown effect in the two practitioner tests found; the one drawdown-tiered crypto preprint has no train/test split and leaves the return/MDD ratio unchanged (4.34 vs 4.33) ([Talyxion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13239)). The capital rules' existing hard halt plus human re-enable is cleaner than any continuous de-risking schedule.

**Implementation facts that bias backtests toward stops.** Freqtrade fills stops "exactly at stoploss price, even if low was lower," assumes low before high, checks stoploss before ROI (so backtests show more stop exits than live), evaluates `custom_stoploss` in backtests with the candle *high* as `current_rate`, and fills exit-signal exits at the *next* candle open — every one of these flatters a tight stop relative to a trend-flip exit ([Freqtrade backtesting](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/); [strategy callbacks](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-callbacks/)). The system's −2.8% result for EMA+ATR stop is therefore an upper bound on the same design live. `minimal_roi` defaults to `{"0": 10}` (effectively off); copied sample configs with `"0": 0.04`-style tables cap trend trades at a few percent ([Freqtrade configuration](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/configuration/)). Kraken supports stop-loss-market on exchange and Freqtrade recommends it, but the on-exchange update interval is 60 s ([Freqtrade stoploss](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/stoploss/)). At $20–$100 the binding floor is `costmin` $0.50 per order, not the lot minimums; vol targeting a 70%-vol asset to 10–20% implies $3–$30 positions, and fees of ≈ $0.02–$0.08 per side on a $20 position are a material fraction of expected profit.

**The current IS Sharpe ≥ 1.0 gate has no power once a few hundred candidates have been scored.** Using the Deflated Sharpe formulas (E[max SR] = σ_trials·[(1−γ)Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γΦ⁻¹(1−1/(Ne))]), a modest cross-trial dispersion of 0.03/day gives an expected best-of-noise Sharpe of ≈ 1.66 annualised at N = 300 and ≈ 1.86 at N = 1,000 ([endgame sharpe.py](https://endgame-ml.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/endgame/utils/sharpe.html); [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). A nightly loop reaches N = 300 in under a year. The minimum track record to reject SR* = 0 at 95% for a true annualised Sharpe of 1.0 is ≈ 992 days; tested against SR* = E[max SR] it is infinite, and a Sharpe-2.0 strategy against N = 300 needs ≈ 23 years. Harvey & Liu's worked example (SR 1.0, 120 months, N = 100, ρ = 0.4) gives haircuts of 52–75% ([CME PDF](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf)); their t > 3 hurdle on three years of daily returns means an annualised Sharpe of ≳ 1.7 *before* multiplicity correction ([NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). None of these are crypto-specific; they are arithmetic.

**Look-ahead dominates every other failure mode in magnitude and cannot be caught statistically.** Gençay's search-aware audit planted a leaky oracle that posted design Sharpe 34.7 and DSR 1.00 and passed PBO — "statistical corrections alone do not catch look-ahead"; only structural exclusion does ([arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734)). Freqtrade's `lookahead-analysis` re-runs a backtest per signal and compares dataframes, but "only signals that actually trigger are verified" (false negatives), pairlist-dependent logic produces false positives, market orders are forced so custom price callbacks are not exercised, and the docs state "removing bias from a strategy usually reduces its performance significantly" ([lookahead-analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/)). `recursive-analysis` checks only `populate_indicators` and `@informative` indicators, compares last-row values at startup counts 199/499/999/1999, and "does not show the effect on actual entries or exits" ([recursive-analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/recursive-analysis/)). Common leaks: `shift(-N)`, `.iloc[]` in populate functions, un-windowed `.mean()/.min()/.max()`, plain `.merge()` of a higher timeframe (use `merge_informative_pair()`, which shifts by one candle), `.resample()` without `label='right'`, `ta.MACD` with signal period 1 ([strategy customization](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-customization/)). The backtester's fill model — entries at open, fills at requested price with zero slippage inside the candle range, stops at exact price, low before high — is mitigated but not removed by `--timeframe-detail` ([Freqtrade backtesting](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)).

**Walk-forward is itself a search, and the 50% OOS/IS gate is roughly the median published decay.** Bailey et al. reject hold-out as a defence because it "ignores N," require disclosure of all trials, and note that T should be about double the observations used to choose a configuration ([PBO paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)). Quantpedia's 355-strategy comparison shows Sharpe falling from mean 1.57 to 1.05 and median 1.18 to 0.66 at the source paper's sample end, so a candidate passing exactly at 50% should be assumed to deliver roughly half again live ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-analysis-of-trading-strategies/)). Every change of window length, step or metric is another trial; the practitioner thresholds (efficiency < 0.5 red flag, OOS/(IS+OOS) ≈ 0.2–0.33) are uncited ([TradeScript](https://tradescript.substack.com/p/walk-forward-optimization-why-your)), and no source states how many OOS windows are needed — with fewer than ~6, the gate is dominated by one or two windows. On 4h bars with multi-day holds, 3-month OOS windows are the minimum for ≥ 20 trades per window.

**The ≤ 6-parameter rule has no primary source; it is a proxy for capping trials.** With k parameters over g grid values, N = g^k; the rule limits N, but the honest rule is to cap *distinct evaluations* and register all of them. The one peer-reviewed plateau paper reports plateau-selected parameters "performed similarly or better on the test set than on the training set" for 2–6-parameter strategies, but its score formula is paywalled ([Harbourfront summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/avoiding-overfitting-searching-for)). Regime decay for crypto specifically has no half-life estimate; the cross-asset evidence (~5%/year additional Sharpe decay for newly published factors, per Falck, Rej & Thesmar as cited by Quantpedia) is the only prior.

**Twenty paper trades cannot support any statistical claim.** The t > 3 hurdle at trade level with n = 20 requires a per-trade Sharpe of 0.67, which is not a meaningful test; Gençay's power calculation says a true Sharpe of 0.6 needs ~11 years OOS to reach t ≈ 2 ([arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)). The paper gate can detect gross implementation failures (unfilled limit orders, wrong fee tier, timing bugs) and should be repurposed to measure them.

**Where LLMs hurt: any role in which the model decides, or in which it sees dated bars.** The arena and replication evidence above is uniformly negative or luck-indistinguishable for LLM decisions. Three independent methods show that backtests of LLM judgement inside the training window are inadmissible: Glasserman & Lin find anonymised headlines scored by GPT-3.5 performed *better* in-sample than named ones (a "distraction effect" from memorised company knowledge), with the gap narrowing out of sample ([arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322v1)); Gao, Jiang & Yan's date-only "Lookahead Propensity" query shows a one-SD rise in LAP adds ~32% to apparent forecast skill pre-cutoff and collapses to zero after it (t = 3.64 vs 1.06) ([arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847)); Look-Ahead-Bench finds bigger models decay *more* (DeepSeek −21.8 pp vs a momentum baseline −2.2 pp), attributed to stronger memorised priors ([arXiv 2601.13770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13770)). For current frontier models the clean window after the cutoff is months, too short to certify a moderate edge. This is why the research-loop prompt in deliverable 03 shows the model schema only, never price series or dated bars.

**Where LLMs plausibly help: cheap hypothesis generation, deflated by trial count.** The positive factor-mining papers share features this system lacks — large validated operator libraries, a LightGBM downstream model absorbing weak factors, Chinese A-share cross-sections of thousands of names, best-of-20 selection — and none has independent replication ([AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v1); [R&D-Agent-Quant](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155v2); [QuantaAlpha](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07085)). The setting closest to this system is Gençay's single-strategy search on US equities and ETFs with a leakage-safe feature registry, trial ledger, DSR, PBO and bootstrap CIs: gpt-4.1's best of 100 proposals went from in-sample Sharpe 1.69 to held-out 0.18 (DSR 0.86); claude-sonnet-5's best went 0.44 → −0.33; five repeated runs were *all* profitable OOS (Sharpe 0.59–0.70) yet DSR 0.15–0.29, "indistinguishable from luck at the recorded trial count"; classic momentum/low-vol/reversal factors also failed while equal-weight buy-and-hold cleared the bar; and runs "settled on the same strategy family" ([arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)). Anic et al.'s best-controlled sentiment tilt adds ~0.25 Sharpe at 10% significance over 15 months after a 512-combination search, and the "basic" prompt beat the "advanced" one ([arXiv 2510.26228](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26228v1)). Crypto-specific LLM ideation evidence: none.

**Scaffolding with evidence is deterministic and statistical; prompt-level scaffolds are plausible but unmeasured.** The components that recur across the rigorous protocols are: structural leakage exclusion (a feature registry in which look-ahead is inexpressible), a ledger counting every evaluation including failures and duplicates as the deflation input, held-out windows with bootstrap CIs, net-of-cost evaluation with cost stress, random and buy-and-hold nulls, pre-registration of a small set of hypotheses (which "faces little deflation"), and independent blind generation before any critique ([Gençay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734); [Alpha Illusion P1–P6](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16895v1); [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18005)). Hypothesis-before-code, originality/complexity regularisers and schema-validated output appear in AlphaAgent, QuantaAlpha and R&D-Agent-Quant but no ablation isolates their OOS contribution. LLM critics are structurally unreliable as gates: GPT-4's recall on its own outputs was 0.945 when humans agreed and 0.425 when they did not, driven by low-perplexity (familiar) text ([Wataoka et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819)); position bias is systematic and repeatable ([Shi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791v1)); ~61% of multi-agent sessions open with deference and < 1% with pushback ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18005)). A critic from a different model family, blind to the proposer's conclusion, rubric-scored and advisory only, is the defensible design. Security: all 15 academic trading agents surveyed were vulnerable to attacks on information sources ([FARSIGHT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19705)); an inline harness cut attack success from 38.3% to 15.0% but "no fool-proof prevention method is known" ([FinHarness](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27333); [OWASP LLM01](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)). For a research-only loop the realistic harm is poisoned hypotheses and wasted budget; mode collapse within a few dozen iterations is the likelier day-to-day failure.

**Kraken's new official bulk archive changes the data decision.** Kraken now publishes a free, complete OHLCVT history for every pair "from its first trade on Kraken through 30 June 2026," refreshed quarterly, at 1/5/15/30/60/240/720/1440-minute intervals, as headerless CSVs (`timestamp, open, high, low, close, volume, trades`) with a `MANIFEST.json` and SHA256 checksums ([Kraken support](https://support.kraken.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047124832-Downloadable-historical-OHLCVT-Open-High-Low-Close-Volume-Trades-data)). Kraken's developer guide still says no bulk dump exists ([docs.kraken.com](https://docs.kraken.com/exchange/guides/general/historical-data)); the support page is newer and more specific. The REST OHLC endpoint returns "up to 720 of the most recent entries" and "older data cannot be retrieved, regardless of `since`" ([Kraken OHLC docs](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/rest-api/get-ohlc-data)), which Freqtrade's docs confirm ("The Kraken API only provides 720 historic candles") and which is why `--dl-trades` has been mandatory ([Freqtrade exchanges](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/exchanges/)). With the 2026Q3 incremental file listed, the gap on 2026-10-14 is ~2 weeks, well within the 720-candle REST window. The archive states no licence; redistribution rights are unknown, and Freqtrade's `kraken_csv` importer is built for Kraken *trades* CSVs, not these candle files, so a small converter to feather is needed.

**The Binance Vision terms make "backtest on Binance, trade on Kraken" a licensing problem.** The Terms of Use PDF (Version 1.0, "Last Updated: August 26, 2026") license the datasets under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (§3.1), permit "algorithmic historical backtesting for purely personal non-production research" (§4.1), prohibit use "for live proprietary trading execution, automated commercial order generation, or signal distribution" (§4.2), prohibit hosting or sub-licensing (§4.3), require derivatives to attribute "Binance Vision" and remain CC BY-NC-SA (§4.5), and state that "any data derived from, calculated with, or extracted from the Datasets," including models and indicators, is governed by the terms (§2.4) ([Binance Vision ToS](https://data.binance.vision/Binance_Vision-Terms_of_Use.pdf)). Whether a strategy promoted from a Binance backtest to live Kraken execution is a "derivative" under §2.4 and §4.2 is a legal-grey question with no opinion found; whether the terms also cover `/api/v3/klines` is unresolved ("associated endpoints" is undefined). Binance exited Canada on 2023-09-30 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/binance-calls-it-quits-in-canada-blames-new-rules)) and blocks API access from Canadian server IPs per Freqtrade. The safe pattern is Apache-2.0 code, data fetched at runtime and never committed, checksums committed, Kraken-native data as the primary and promotion-path source, and Binance only as a private validation feed with attribution — and a revisit of the ADR that chose Binance for backtesting.

**Quality hazards are venue-local wicks, omitted bars, mixed timestamp units and silently replaced files.** Binance.US printed BTC at $8,200 on 2021-10-21 while Kraken's thin book printed $55,500 against ~$64,000 elsewhere ([K33](https://k33.com/research/archive/articles/flash-crash-on-binance-us)); on 2025-10-10 ATOM, IOTX and ENJ "briefly hit zero" on Binance ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/why-did-some-altcoins-on-binance-crash-to-zero)), and whether those prints remain in the archive is unconfirmed. Binance archive files "may be replaced as a result of recently discovered issues" and spot timestamps switch from 13-digit milliseconds to 16-digit microseconds on 2025-01-01 ([README](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data)). Kraken and Coinbase omit intervals with no trades. The validation pipeline that follows: normalise units by digit count, assert every 4h open-time is a multiple of 14,400,000 ms, reindex to the full grid with inserted bars flagged `synthetic=True`, check OHLC sanity, flag `(high−low)/close` outliers against a rolling MAD and cross-check against a second venue, flag any bar where |close_binance/close_kraken − 1| > 0.5%, re-verify checksums on every refresh, and drop Kraken's uncommitted last REST candle.

**Basis risk is in liquidity, not in the price level.** Binance's own BTCUSD–BTCUSDT premium averaged ~0.034% (peak 0.103%) over a June–July 2025 window ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/insights/bitcoins-climb-past-110k-to-within-2-of-ath-tightens-spread-between-usd-and-usdt-pairs/)), negligible for 4h bars except during stablecoin stress (USDT at $0.995 in June 2023 ([Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/the-data-behind-tethers-depeg))). But Kraken BTC-USD spreads are the tightest among USD venues while SOL-USDT shows the widest dispersion on Binance ([Kaiko spread cheat-sheet](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/a-cheatsheet-for-bid-ask-spreads)), and Binance USDT volume is no proxy for Kraken USD depth in SOL/LINK/XRP/LTC: any slippage model calibrated on Binance will be optimistic on Kraken. Calibrate from Kraken's own `volume` and `trades` columns.
