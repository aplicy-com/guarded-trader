# Reading List: The 24 Sources That Matter Most for This System

Purpose: the sources from the research notes ranked by usefulness to a Kraken spot, long-only, 4h/1d Freqtrade system with an LLM research loop — one paragraph each on what the source shows, why it matters here, its grade, and the link.
Date: 2026-10-14
How to read the grades: **A** = primary paper, official documentation or live parameter; replicated/after-cost where it is an empirical claim. **B** = single solid study or maintained open-source documentation. **C** = vendor, practitioner, preprint-only or in-sample. Where a note says the full text could not be fetched, that is stated.

---

**1. Kraken Pro fee schedule (official page, fetched 2026-10-14).** Shows Tier 1 (< $2,500 30-day volume) at 0.40% maker / 0.80% taker, Tier 2 at 0.30/0.60, with Instant Buy volume excluded from tiering. It matters because the system's gates were calibrated at 0.16/0.26, a $100 account is permanently Tier 1, and at 0.40/0.80 a round trip costs 0.8–1.6% before slippage, so the required average gross edge per trade for a 40% fee ratio becomes 2–4%. Verify the tier in the live account before trusting either figure; the effective date (9 July 2026) comes only from a third party. Grade A (vendor page) — [kraken.com/features/fee-schedule](https://www.kraken.com/features/fee-schedule); third-party context [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/kraken-fees-explained).

**2. Freqtrade backtesting documentation.** Lists every fill assumption the backtester makes: entries at candle open, orders filled at the requested price with no slippage if inside the candle range, exit signals filled at the next open, stoploss checked before ROI, low assumed before high, stops filled exactly at the stop price "even if the low went lower," `custom_stoploss` evaluated at the candle high, current rather than historic exchange limits, and the ccxt default fee unless `--fee` is set. Every one of these flatters tight stops and understates costs; the system's −2.8% EMA+ATR result is an upper bound on the same design live. Grade A — [freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/).

**3. Freqtrade `lookahead-analysis`, `recursive-analysis` and the "common mistakes" section.** Document the two shipped leak detectors and their blind spots ("only signals that actually trigger are verified"; `recursive-analysis` "does not show the effect on actual entries or exits"; "a negative result … does not guarantee" no bias), plus the specific constructions that leak (`shift(-N)`, `.iloc[]`, un-windowed `.mean()`, plain `.merge()`, `.resample()` without `label='right'`). The docs also state that "removing bias from a strategy usually reduces its performance significantly," which is the right prior for every too-good backtest the loop produces. Grade A — [lookahead-analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/); [recursive-analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/recursive-analysis/); [strategy customization](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-customization/).

**4. Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting."** Defines CSCV/PBO (S = 16 blocks, 12,870 train/test combinations, reject PBO > 0.05), proves hold-out "ignores N," and sets the disclosure rule the registry implements: all trials must be disclosed, optimisers contribute only final outcomes, and T should be about double the observations used to choose a configuration. It is the method the loop needs to measure its own selection bias. Grade A — [davidhbailey.com PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf).

**5. Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio."** Corrects a Sharpe ratio for the number of trials and for skew/kurtosis; the expected-maximum-Sharpe formula gives ≈ 1.66 annualised for 300 noise trials and ≈ 1.86 for 1,000 at modest trial dispersion, which is why the IS Sharpe ≥ 1.0 gate has no power once the loop has run for a year. The SSRN abstract is open; an open-source implementation of the formulas is in the `endgame` package. Grade A (paper) / B (implementation) — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [endgame sharpe.py](https://endgame-ml.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/endgame/utils/sharpe.html).

**6. Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting."** Gives the haircut procedure (Sharpe → t → p → Bonferroni/Holm/BHY → haircut Sharpe) with a worked example: SR 1.0 over 120 months with 100 trials is haircut 52% (BHY) to 75% (Bonferroni), and a flat 50% haircut is "too lenient for SR below ~0.4 and too harsh above ~1.0." All haircuts are before costs. R code is in the quantstrat sandbox. Grade A — [CME-hosted PDF](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf); [quantstrat R](https://rdrr.io/github/braverock/quantstrat/src/sandbox/Harvey_Liu_Backtesting/50a170e4e37da951ea1192888418c82a/Haircut_SR%20with%20sample_random_multests.R).

**7. Gençay, "What survives honest evaluation? Leakage-safe, search-aware assessment of LLM-driven trading strategy discovery."** The closest published analogue to this system's loop: an LLM composes strategies from a registry in which look-ahead is inexpressible, every evaluation is logged, and DSR/PBO/bootstrap CIs are computed from the ledger. Result: 0 of ~200 LLM-proposed strategies certified; five repeated runs all profitable OOS yet DSR 0.15–0.29; a planted leaky oracle posted Sharpe 34.7 and DSR 1.00 and passed PBO; runs converged on one strategy family; a true Sharpe 0.6 needs ~11 years to certify. Single author, but the most rigorous protocol found. Grade B — [arXiv 2608.27734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734).

**8. Clare, Seaton, Smith & Thomas, "Breaking into the Blackbox: Trend Following, Stop Losses and the Frequency of Trading."** S&P 500 daily 1988–2011 at 0.2% per buy and per sell — the closest cost regime to Tier-1 Kraken in the literature. Finds stop-loss and trailing-stop overlays on MA and breakout rules add nothing or hurt (12% trailing stop: Sharpe 0.56 → 0.54; 50/200 crossover with stop: 10.62% → 7.45%), monthly evaluation beats daily, and "a change of trend is the best stop loss." Directly on point for a system that lost with an EMA cross plus ATR stop. Grade B (single index) — [City Research Online PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/17842/8/BLACKBOX%20%20%20SSRN-id2126476.pdf).

**9. Kaminski & Lo, "When Do Stop-Loss Rules Stop Losses?"** Proves that under a random walk with positive drift any stop lowers expected return, that a stop adds value only when serial correlation at the stop's horizon exceeds the Sharpe ratio at that frequency, and that short-horizon stops on S&P futures have negative stopping premiums over large parameter ranges even before costs (κ = 0). It is the theoretical reason a 4h ATR stop on BTC was a cost, not a protection. Grade A (theory) / B (empirics) — [MIT DSpace PDF](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf).

**10. Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison & van Hemert, "The Impact of Volatility Targeting."** 60+ assets with costs: vol targeting raises equity/credit Sharpe (~0.40 → 0.48–0.51), consistently cuts extreme returns and vol-of-vol, costs at most ~5 round trips per year, but has negligible effect on currencies and commodities — and crypto is not in the sample. It is the best-replicated risk tool in the evidence base and the mechanism (vol scaling behaves like time-series momentum in assets with a leverage effect) is the hypothesis behind candidate C04; whether BTC behaves like equities or commodities is unknown. Grade A (non-crypto) — [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf).

**11. Han, Kang & Ryu, "Time Series and Cross Sectional Momentum in the Cryptocurrency Market."** The best after-cost, daily-marked crypto TSMOM study found: 2013–2023, 15 bp per trade, best long/short rule (28-day lookback, 5-day hold) Sharpe 1.51 vs ~0.85 market, invested ~48% of the time, long-only versions "mostly beat the market," short-only mostly lose; pairs chosen ex post with no OOS test. The source of candidate C01's lookback range and utilisation estimate. Grade B — [AUT ACFR PDF](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf).

**12. Liu & Tsyvinski, "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency."** BTC-only time-series tests 2011–2018: past 1-week return predicts the next 1–3 weeks (t = 3.7–4.5); spreads roughly halve when 2011–12 is excluded; the daily effect is bootstrap-fragile; no costs anywhere. The canonical reference for weekly-scale crypto momentum, to be read with its gross-returns caveat. Grade B — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf).

**13. Grobys, Kolari, Sandretto, Shahzad & Äijö, "Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments."** Top-30 coins 2016–2023: momentum 1.74%/week to July 2020 (10% significance), negative and insignificant afterwards; one observation = 37% of compounded return; tail exponents 2.45–2.87 (variance undefined); vol management cuts kurtosis but not tail risk. The clearest post-2020 decay evidence and the empirical justification for the "no trade > 30% of profit" gate and the block-bootstrap drawdown test. Grade B (open access) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9).

**14. Mercik, Zaremba & Demir, "Crypto Factor Zoo."** 36 predictors, 565 coins, 2018–2024, net of 0.1–0.4%/trade: 7-day momentum falls from 100% to 29% annualised at 0.4%/trade and weakens from 2021; the only factor persisting across both halves is bid-ask spread. The second independent post-2021 decay split, and a quantitative picture of how much cost drag a weekly-rebalanced rule absorbs. Grade B — [ICM PDF](https://open.icm.edu.pl/bitstreams/21636cf6-61df-4fb5-9ae4-4195d592cd26/download).

**15. Profit Mirage (arXiv 2510.07920) and The Alpha Illusion (arXiv 2605.16895).** Two independent reproductions of the LLM-agent trading papers: 50–72% total-return decay and 51–62% Sharpe decay when tested after the model's knowledge cutoff; frontier models answered ~85–93% of historical price/trend/event questions correctly; TradingAgents' Sharpe 0.43 → 0.22 after costs. Together they establish that any backtest in which an LLM sees dated data inside its training window is inadmissible — the reason the research-loop prompt is schema-only. Grade B (borderline A: independent teams, costs included) — [Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1); [Alpha Illusion](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16895v1).

**16. Gao, Jiang & Yan, "Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts" and Glasserman & Lin, "Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated by GPT Sentiment Analysis."** The date-only "Lookahead Propensity" query shows a one-SD rise in LAP adds ~32% to apparent forecast skill before the cutoff and nothing after (t = 3.64 vs 1.06); anonymising company identifiers *improved* in-sample performance, revealing a memorised-knowledge "distraction" effect. Both give the loop a pre-flight contamination test and the rule that prompt-level masking must be re-tested, not assumed. Grade B — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847); [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322v1).

**17. nof1 Alpha Arena coverage (Seasons 1 and 1.5).** Live, real-money LLM trading: four of six models lost 42–59% on crypto perps in 17 days; seven of eight lost in the equity season; one model placed 1,418 trades in two weeks under the same prompt that produced 158 from another; the organiser concluded autonomous LLM trading "isn't a workable path yet." No fee totals, no drawdowns, no third-party reconciliation, and the venue charged 0.045% taker — 9–18× cheaper than Kraken Tier 1. Grade B (live, public, n ≈ 6–8) — [ForkLog S1](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/); [Business Standard / Bloomberg](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/ai-bots-auditioning-for-wall-street-trading-are-mostly-losing-money-126050701793_1.html); [Blockworks snapshot](https://empire-blockworks.beehiiv.com/p/ai-trades-on-hyperliquid).

**18. Kraken downloadable historical OHLCVT data (support page, updated 2026-10-12).** Announces a free, official, complete per-pair history "from its first trade on Kraken through 30 June 2026" with quarterly incremental zips, 1/5/15/30/60/240/720/1440-minute intervals, headerless CSVs with a manifest and SHA256 checksums, and the caveat that empty intervals are omitted; no licence is stated. It removes the original reason for backtesting on Binance (the 720-candle REST cap) and should trigger a revisit of that ADR. Grade A (vendor page) — [Kraken support](https://support.kraken.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047124832-Downloadable-historical-OHLCVT-Open-High-Low-Close-Volume-Trades-data); REST limits at [Get OHLC Data](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/rest-api/get-ohlc-data).

**19. Binance Vision Terms of Use (PDF, Version 1.0, last updated 26 August 2026).** Licenses the data.binance.vision datasets under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, permits "algorithmic historical backtesting for purely personal non-production research," prohibits "live proprietary trading execution," hosting and sub-licensing, and states that models and indicators "derived from" the datasets are governed by the terms. Whether a strategy backtested on Binance and traded on Kraken is a prohibited derivative is a legal-grey question with no opinion found; the conservative path is Kraken-native data for anything on the promotion path. Grade A (primary document) — [Binance Vision ToS PDF](https://data.binance.vision/Binance_Vision-Terms_of_Use.pdf); archive format at [binance-public-data README](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data).

**20. Kraken `AssetPairs` endpoint and minimum-order-size support page.** The live API (fetched 2026-10-14) gives `costmin` $0.50 for all five pairs and `ordermin` 0.00006 BTC / 0.05 SOL / 4 XRP / 0.35 LINK / 0.08 LTC; the support page says 0.0001 BTC and "the equivalent value of 1 USD" for crypto-to-crypto. At $20–$100 with vol-scaled sizing the `costmin` floor binds and fees become a material fraction of expected profit; re-query before go-live and treat the API as authoritative. Grade A (live parameter) — [Kraken AssetPairs](https://api.kraken.com/0/public/AssetPairs?pair=XBTUSD,SOLUSD,XRPUSD,LINKUSD,LTCUSD); [Kraken support](https://support.kraken.com/articles/205893708-Minimum-order-size-volume-for-trading).

**21. Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, "Crypto Carry" (BIS Working Paper 1087) and the CEPR/VoxEU column.** Crypto carry averages 7–10%/yr and spikes above 40–60%, driven by retail leveraged-long demand; "high carry predicts future price crashes," and a 10% rise in standardised carry predicts a 22% rise in sell liquidations relative to open interest over the next month. No spot-return timing test is reported, so the supported use for a spot-only system is a "do not add longs" crowding flag (candidate C09), not an entry signal. Grade B — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm); [CEPR column](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/crypto-carry-market-segmentation-and-price-distortions-digital-asset-markets).

**22. OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 (LLM01/LLM05/LLM06) and Anthropic's prompt-injection guidance; FARSIGHT SoK.** The standards consensus for a loop that ingests fetched content: isolate and label untrusted content, keep it out of the system prompt, validate outputs with deterministic code, least privilege with the application holding credentials, human approval for irreversible actions, red-team before deployment, and "no fool-proof prevention method is known." FARSIGHT finds all 15 academic trading agents surveyed vulnerable and 80% failing a robustness metric. Grade A (standards) / B (FARSIGHT, abstract only) — [OWASP LLM01](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/); [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/mitigating-jailbreaks-prompt-injections); [FARSIGHT arXiv 2609.19705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19705).

**23. Wataoka, Takahashi & Ri, "Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" and Chen et al., "Diversity Collapse in Multi-Agent LLM Systems."** GPT-4's recall on its own outputs was 0.945 when humans agreed and 0.425 when they did not, driven by low-perplexity text; multi-agent ideation's diversity-utilisation ratio fell from 1.03 to 0.47 going from 3 to 7 agents, with ~61% deference openings and < 1% pushback. Together they justify a critic from a different model family, blind and advisory only, and independent blind generation before any discussion. Grade B — [arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819); [arXiv 2604.18005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18005).

**24. Regulator and exchange account-level studies of retail trader outcomes (Barber et al.; Chague et al.; SEBI; BIS).** Fewer than 1% of ~360,000 Taiwanese day traders consistently profitable net of costs; 3% of Brazilian futures day-traders with any net profit; 93% of >10 million Indian F&O traders losing, with "most of the profits … generated by larger entities that used trading algorithms"; ~75% of retail bitcoin-app users across 95 countries losing. These set the prior — low single digits — against which every claimed result, including the loop's own, must be discounted. Grade A — [Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trade%20040330.pdf); [Chague et al. SSRN](https://ssrn.com/abstract=3423101); [SEBI via Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/fo-frenzy-over-93-retail-investors-lost-average-rs-2-lakh-in-three-years-says-sebi); [BIS via The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2023-02-20-majority-of-bitcoin-retail-investors-lost-money-in-the-last-seven-years-bis-213372).
